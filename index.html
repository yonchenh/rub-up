Paper of the day

Thermomechanical devulcanization of highly filled EPDM rubber waste — Rodrigues et al., Materials, 2026

  • In short: Heat and shear in a mixer, with or without a disulfide agent (DBD, a cousin of your DPDS).
  • Key result: Best was 86 % devulcanized with 1 phr DBD. But with no agent at all, they already got 74 %.
  • For you: The no-agent effect can be big. That's why your Method A blank is the right first step.

Also worth a look

  • Review: devulcanization of sulfur-cured EPDM — the field still has no shared way to measure devulcanization.
  • Nitrile rubber devulcanized with TBAF — a mild route that mainly cuts polysulfidic links.

Keep going

A blank run can feel like "no result", but it's often the number that explains the rest.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In the EPDM paper, how much devulcanization came from heat and shear alone, with no agent?

Heat and shear alone did most of the work (74 %), so blank runs matter.

2. What happened when they used more agent (3 phr DBD instead of 1 phr)?

It dropped from 86 % to about 70 %. More agent is not always better.

3. Which crosslinks did the mild TBAF route cut most easily?

Long S–S bridges are the weakest bonds, so they break first. It worked best on conventional cures, which have many of them.

Core topics

  • Devulcanization of ground tire rubber (GTR) / waste tyre rubber
  • Supercritical CO2 (scCO2) devulcanization, scCO2 swelling and diffusion in rubber
  • Chemical devulcanization agents: diphenyl disulfide (DPDS) and other disulfides, amines, thiols
  • Thermo-mechanical, microwave, ultrasound and biological devulcanization
  • Solvent / Soxhlet extraction pre- and post-treatment of rubber (toluene, acetone, other solvents)
  • Model compounds: NR, SBR, BR/PBR and their blends; sulfur vulcanization recipes (S, CBS, ZnO, stearic acid)
  • Sulfur crosslink chemistry: mono-, di- and polysulfidic crosslinks; selective S-S vs C-C scission
  • Crosslink density: equilibrium swelling, Flory-Rehner, Horikx analysis, sol fraction
  • Analysis of (de)vulcanized rubber: FTIR, CHNS/O elemental analysis (sulfur), TGA, ICP (Zn), DSC, NMR
  • Reuse of devulcanized rubber in new compounds; properties of revulcanized blends
  • End-of-life tyres: EU policy, circular economy, industry news, life cycle assessment
