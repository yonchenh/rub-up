Chemistry Wed 11 Nov 2026

Wednesday 11 Nov · Chemistry

Paper of the day

Sulfur-bridge topology and mechanics in natural rubber — Wei et al., Journal of Molecular Modeling, 2026

  • In short: They simulated NR networks with one-, two- and many-sulfur bridges at three sulfur levels.
  • Key result: Network shape matters more than sulfur amount; long polysulfidic bridges give more defects and a softer network.
  • For you: It shows why cutting polysulfidic links first changes a network so much.

Also worth a look

  • Thermochemistry of vulcanization and devulcanized NR — a re-cure heat peak can separate true devulcanization from reclaim.
  • Devulcanization parameters of EPDM/GTR compounds — Horikx analysis (a plot of scission type) shows when S–S cutting turns into C–C cutting.

Keep going

Six months in, you can already read these papers as a chemist who has the recipes in hand.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In the Wei study, what explains the mechanical response better than sulfur amount alone?

Mono-, di- and polysulfidic bridges build different network shapes at the same sulfur level.

2. Why might devulcanized rubber show a heat peak when re-cured?

Selective cutting keeps the polymer chains intact, so they can re-vulcanize.

3. A sample lies near the S–S/C–S curve on a Horikx plot. What does that suggest?

Near the S–S curve means sulfur links broke, not the backbone.

Tuesday 10 Nov · Analysis

Paper of the day

Rubber compounds from devulcanized GTR — Görbe et al., Polymers, 2024

  • In short: They measured crosslink density by swelling, sol fraction (the part that dissolves) by Soxhlet, and Horikx plots.
  • Key result: Crosslink density fell about 60 %, sol fraction reached about 30 %.
  • For you: Horikx tells you if bonds were cut selectively or the chains were chopped at random.

Also worth a look

  • Selective GTR devulcanization in a twin-screw extruder — mild settings cut crosslinks selectively (K = 0.94).
  • Sulfur removal from rubber, tracked by FTIR — C–S and S–O bands vanished after treatment.

Keep going

Six months in, you already run the same measurements the published papers lean on.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. On a Horikx plot, what does a point near the "selective" curve mean?

Selective means crosslinks break before the main chains do.

2. What does the sol fraction measure?

A high sol fraction with little crosslink loss points to chain scission.

3. In the extruder paper, what happened at higher temperature and screw speed?

Harsher conditions chop the chains, so the sol fraction rises.

Monday 9 Nov · Methods

Paper of the day

Pre-soaking scrap tyre rubber before devulcanization — Bera et al., Polymer Engineering & Science, 2026

  • In short: They soaked tyre crumb in a solvent with TMTD (a disulfide agent), then milled it.
  • Key result: Devulcanization took 21.5 min instead of about 60, using roughly 60 % less energy.
  • For you: A swelling pre-soak can carry a disulfide like DPDS deep into rubber before scCO2 treatment.

Also worth a look

  • Pilot-scale scCO2 devulcanization with DPDS — scCO2 plus DPDS scaled up; 1.5 phr sulfur worked best for re-curing.
  • Green solvent plus bacteria devulcanize tyre rubber — crosslink density fell 43.6 %; Horikx showed mixed scission.

Keep going

Measuring week is where a reaction turns into evidence. Each careful number counts.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. Why did Bera et al. pre-soak the rubber in a solvent with TMTD?

Swelling opens the network, so TMTD can reach crosslinks before milling.

2. How long did the pre-soaked batch need, versus about an hour normally?

21.5 min, against 57–60 min for conventional batches.

3. Horikx analysis showed "mixed" scission in the bacteria paper. What does that mean?

Points between the two Horikx curves mean both bond types were cut.

Classic Fri 06 Nov 2026

Friday 6 Nov · Classic

Paper of the day

Chemical recycling of sulfur-cured NR using supercritical CO2 — Kojima et al., Green Chemistry, 2004

  • In short: They devulcanized sulfur-cured natural rubber in scCO2 with diphenyl disulfide (DPDS).
  • Key result: Sol (dissolvable part) rose with CO2 pressure, mostly above the critical point.
  • For you: This is the founding paper for the scCO2 + DPDS route.

Also worth a look

  • scCO2 + DPDS on natural rubber industry waste — about 90 % devulcanized, with almost no degradation.
  • Review: science and technology of rubber reclamation — recipes, chemistry and a scission model in one read.

This week

  • Mon: heat-and-shear EPDM devulcanization
  • Tue: green solvent for tyre crumb
  • Wed: bacteria eating tyre rubber
  • Thu: tyre routes ranked on cost and climate

Keep going

Your route stands on twenty years of scCO2 work, and you're adding the next step.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In Kojima's work, what happened to the sol fraction above CO2's critical pressure?

scCO2 helps DPDS get into the rubber, so more chains are freed.

2. What is the "sol" fraction of a devulcanized rubber?

Sol is the freed, soluble rubber; the gel is the network left behind.

3. What does Horikx analysis (sol fraction vs crosslink density) tell you?

Crosslink scission gives little sol; main-chain scission gives much more.

Thursday 5 Nov · Big picture

Paper of the day

Valorising end-of-life tyres in Ireland: cost and climate — Pati et al., Sustainable Production and Consumption, 2026

  • In short: They compared five routes for Irish waste tyres, from burning to pyrolysis (heating without oxygen).
  • Key result: Thermo-mechanical devulcanisation won on climate and money, but needs about €120 M to build.
  • For you: Devulcanized rubber beats burning tyres for energy, even on cost.

Also worth a look

  • Closed-loop tyre recycling with devulcanized rubber: a life cycle study — weighs climate gains against durability when rubber returns to tyres.
  • Continental puts recovered carbon black in conveyor belts — tyre-derived filler is now in real industrial products.

Keep going

A cost-and-climate study just ranked devulcanization first. Selective chemistry like yours makes it even better.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In the Irish study, which route scored best on climate and economics?

It had the best environmental performance and the highest economic viability.

2. In "closed-loop" tyre recycling, where does the old rubber end up?

Closed loop means the material goes back into the same kind of product.

3. How is Bolder's recovered carbon black made?

Bolder heats waste rubber without oxygen, then recovers the carbon black.

Wednesday 4 Nov · Chemistry

Paper of the day

Bacteria slowly break down waste tyre rubber — Shukla et al., RSC Advances, 2026

  • In short: A soil bacterium (Gordonia) grew on ground tyre rubber for 28 days.
  • Key result: Bulk sulfur (CHNS) fell about a third, but crosslink density fell only about 10 %.
  • For you: Less sulfur or more sol fraction does not prove S–S bonds broke.

Also worth a look

  • Copper salt makes sulfur-cured NR recyclable — controlling S–S swapping kept ~85 % strength at 90 % reclaim.
  • From waste rubber to value: polybutadiene chemistry — a short tour of ways to chemically upgrade BR waste.

Keep going

Every careful blank you run now makes your DPDS results easier to trust later.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In the bacteria paper, which bonds did the authors mainly blame for the breakdown?

They link it to an enzyme (Lcp) that cuts the polyisoprene backbone, not the crosslinks.

2. What does a Horikx plot help you tell apart?

It compares sol fraction with crosslink loss to show which bonds broke.

3. What does the copper salt (CuMA) do in the recyclable NR paper?

Copper helps control disulfide exchange, so the network can be reshaped and reused.

Tuesday 3 Nov · Analysis

Paper of the day

Green solvent devulcanizes and detoxifies worn-out tyres — Shukla et al., Results in Engineering, 2026

  • In short: They treated tyre crumb with a green solvent, then checked it with CHNS, swelling, Horikx and TGA.
  • Key result: Zinc fell 90 %. Sulfur removal was 31 % by CHNS, but 51 % by EDS (surface X-ray).
  • For you: Sulfur numbers depend on the method, so always say which one you used.

Also worth a look

  • Amine-assisted devulcanized tyre rubber for asphalt — sol fraction (dissolvable part) rose from 9 % to 29 %.
  • NMR, swelling and rheometry compared on NBR — each method sees the network differently; use them together.

Keep going

Careful analysis turns a quiet blank run into a reference the whole project can lean on.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In the green-solvent paper, which method gave the higher sulfur removal?

EDS looks only at the surface (about 51 %); CHNS burns the whole sample (about 31 %).

2. What does a Horikx analysis help you tell apart?

It compares crosslink loss with sol fraction to see what broke.

3. Sol fraction rises after devulcanization. What does that mean?

The sol is the dissolvable part; it grows when the network opens up.

Methods Mon 02 Nov 2026

Monday 2 Nov · Methods

Paper of the day

Thermomechanical devulcanization of highly filled EPDM rubber waste — Rodrigues et al., Materials, 2026

  • In short: Heat and shear in a mixer, with or without a disulfide agent (DBD, a cousin of your DPDS).
  • Key result: Best was 86 % devulcanized with 1 phr DBD. But with no agent at all, they already got 74 %.
  • For you: The no-agent effect can be big. That's why your Method A blank is the right first step.

Also worth a look

  • Review: devulcanization of sulfur-cured EPDM — the field still has no shared way to measure devulcanization.
  • Nitrile rubber devulcanized with TBAF — a mild route that mainly cuts polysulfidic links.

Keep going

A blank run can feel like "no result", but it's often the number that explains the rest.

Quiz

Tap an answer to check yourself.

1. In the EPDM paper, how much devulcanization came from heat and shear alone, with no agent?

Heat and shear alone did most of the work (74 %), so blank runs matter.

2. What happened when they used more agent (3 phr DBD instead of 1 phr)?

It dropped from 86 % to about 70 %. More agent is not always better.

3. Which crosslinks did the mild TBAF route cut most easily?

Long S–S bridges are the weakest bonds, so they break first. It worked best on conventional cures, which have many of them.

Core topics

  • Devulcanization of ground tire rubber (GTR) / waste tyre rubber
  • Supercritical CO2 (scCO2) devulcanization, scCO2 swelling and diffusion in rubber
  • Chemical devulcanization agents: diphenyl disulfide (DPDS) and other disulfides, amines, thiols
  • Thermo-mechanical, microwave, ultrasound and biological devulcanization
  • Solvent / Soxhlet extraction pre- and post-treatment of rubber (toluene, acetone, other solvents)
  • Model compounds: NR, SBR, BR/PBR and their blends; sulfur vulcanization recipes (S, CBS, ZnO, stearic acid)
  • Sulfur crosslink chemistry: mono-, di- and polysulfidic crosslinks; selective S-S vs C-C scission
  • Crosslink density: equilibrium swelling, Flory-Rehner, Horikx analysis, sol fraction
  • Analysis of (de)vulcanized rubber: FTIR, CHNS/O elemental analysis (sulfur), TGA, ICP (Zn), DSC, NMR
  • Reuse of devulcanized rubber in new compounds; properties of revulcanized blends
  • End-of-life tyres: EU policy, circular economy, industry news, life cycle assessment
